First, write the scene as a short screenplay.

- Camera angle and shot size.
- Character position, pose, expression, and gaze.
- Foreground, background, and environmental details.
- Lighting direction and color palette.
- Exact dialogue and speaker.
- Speech-bubble placement.
- Continuity with the previous and next panels.

Each panel must advance the story, reveal information, establish atmosphere, or communicate an important emotion.

Vary panel sizes naturally. Use close-ups for emotional moments, wider panels for establishing locations, and large cinematic panels for important discoveries.

6. ARABIC SPEECH BUBBLES

All dialogue must be in natural, correctly spelled Arabic.

- Use clear Arabic typography.
- Apply right-to-left text direction.
- Create white speech bubbles with clean black outlines.
- Use rounded bubbles for normal dialogue and carefully shaped burst bubbles for shouting or surprise.
- Position each bubble near its speaker.
- Keep text readable on narrow mobile screens.
- Leave sufficient padding around the text.
- Never cover a character's face or an important visual detail.
- Preserve the exact approved dialogue without inventing additional sentences.
- Use narration boxes only when needed.

For reliable Arabic text rendering, create the artwork and empty speech bubbles first, then insert the verified Arabic text using a proper Arabic font renderer with right-to-left shaping.

Do not generate fake Arabic lettering, random symbols, garbled text, or unreadable pseudo-letters.
